February 13, 2026
In writing we care about showing what's important because the reader interprets and makes up a story that may be different from our initial intent.
Dramaturgy and dramatic writing are not the artistic output but rather an intermediate piece for actors (for example) through which they will perform the final creative output.
Conflict is interesting.
The premise con conflict that's about to happen can create conflict or tension in the audience.
The screenwriter can use conflict to reveal information on a character.
The audience routes for the character with most conflict.
The characters should be subjected to the most amount of conflict, because that's what the audience wants.
Conflict not involving the protagonist won't affect the audience.
Pauses between conflicts are needed but too long of a break will be boring to the audience.
Referring to a character by name instead of their role will sell better the idea.


The professor suggests to stick to the present tense to avoid jumping around tenses.
Also suggests to take notes while listening to other's writings.


A conflict is as interesting as the "stakes" are valuable.
The stakes are essentially what is there to lose for a given character.
The style/writing style does not change the drama.

Characters

Single characters are much more interesting than groups of people, because they allow the audience to attach and project.
Every character has a goal:

conscious → a desire, usually external
unconscious → a need, usually internal


Descriptions of a character are meaningless. The character's approach to situations describes them better.
Changing a character's personality or approach could make the character more interesting.
A structure for this is the transformation arc (or character arc).
The character can have a fatal flaw that impedes the necessary change from happening.


The antagonist generates obstacles for the protagonist.
A great antagonist is the materialization of what the protagonist doesn't want to face.
A great example is Batman and the Joker.


In a story, nuance is hard to clarify and features are hard to define.
It is best to start with a simple narrative that develops nuance, instead of having a complex starting that converges to a simple conclusion.

Monster character is one that has very little depth, is very two dimentional and straight forward.
The meaning of a film is referred to as the theme or as the controlling idea.

Understanding the meaning of a creative work is important for the audience to connect to it on a different level.

The author makes the meaning clearest and most valuable when they are able to present his argument while giving voice to the counter argument on the chosen issue.
For example a movie against racism would be more meaningful if the point of view of the racist is shown and not solely demonized.

The style of a movie can help to promote the controlling idea.

To convey the controlling idea, show your argument and the polar opposite. E.g. perfectionism contrasted by the most imperfect protagonist.

Formatting

Always use monospace fonts.
The verticality of the page is the passage of time. A page is 60 movie seconds.


Scene Heading

Header for every scene, describing place and time (roughly).
It is required whenever changing to another scene.
It's composed by

  • INT or EXT to indicate interior or exterior
  • . (period) as separator
  • The actual location name or a supposed location. Any sublocation is written after the location separated by a comma
  • - (dash) as separator
  • DAY or NIGHT to indicate the time of day
  • optional DAWN or DUSK to further specify the time of day
  • optional CONTINUOUS to indicate a scene that continues from the previous

If we're going back and forth between two scenes we can use INTERCUT TO: scene name to avoid declaring the scene heading before every action and dialogue.


Actions

An action are the paragraph that describe only what is perceivable.
All actions are always in present tense.
Every paragraph represents a different camera shot.
The first time a character appears, its name is always in upper case. Professor suggests to put in parenthesis an age extimate as well as a brief description for appearance, presentation and mood as well. This looks like the following:
CHARACTER (50) tall, well dressed, and upbeat mood.


The character name is a reference to the casting actor, so it must be kept the same throughout the script. So always refer to the character by name.
The text can be bold, italic, UPPER CASE or underlined to give emphasis. Use these decorators sparingly if not at all. I would suggest limiting the decorated text to as few words as possible.
Underlining serves the purpose of giving the editor the inication to show a location that is underlined.


Dialogue

Dialogue is composed by

  • character name in upper case - the name is the same as the one used in action optional (O.S.) (Off screen) or (V.O.) (Voice Over) after the character's name
  • optional parenthetical
  • Actual dialogue
If a dialogue in the script is interrupted by action while it is still ongoing, the next dialogue for the same character, right after the action, will have (CONT'D) after their name to indicate that it never stopped.


Scene change

The transition to another scene can be specified but it is hardly ever used.
At the end of a scene and before the next is declared, the transition can be specified by writing
FADE TO:
or
CUT TO:
on the right side of the page.


Rules

  • Storywriters shall not waste the reader's time and fantasy with details that do not serve the story.
  • Conflict
    • is made from character + goal + obstacle
    • shall be motivated
    • shall grow with intention and nonlinearity throughout the story
    • shall not be unjustified or unnecessary
    • shall grow to not get the audience accustomed to it
  • The backstory shall not be more interesting than the one being told.

Definitions & terms

Timelock → imposing a time limit on a scene. The aspect of time passing heightens the stakes
Characterization → the process of making a character unique by traits, personality, motivations
Transformation arc → the internal journey of a character changing over the course of a story
Fatal flaw → a character's trait or characteristic that stucks with them and is decisive in some, many or all choices the character faces
theme | controlling idea → the theme for europeans and the controlling idea for the americans, it is one phrase expressing the meaning of the movie and the author's point of view on an issue
parenthetical → In the dialogue of a script, it is a description for the delivery of the line. It goes in a new line after the character's name and is in parenthesis

Aristotel divides stories in 3 acts: beginning, middle, end. John Yorke says that all stories can be broke into 5 sections:

  1. equilibrium
  2. disruption
  3. escalation
  4. crisis
  5. resolution


The plot in storytelling is called fabula.
The story is the way the fabula is presented, it unfolds and how it is told.
A story allows for manipulations to the presentation of the fabula, such as

  • point of view or perspective of the events
  • time manipulation - making events last longer, shorter or freezing events during narration
  • sequential ordering - narrating events non sequentially
  • span - how much time passes between events

The story does not include the meaning and interpretation.

The narrative agent


A narrative text is any text in which an agent (writer) conveys to an addressee (reader) a story by a medium (book, movie, building, etc).
Mieke Bal thinks of narrative text having 3 layers

  • text → the expressed discourse
  • story → the content, events and actors
  • fabula → the actual events that took place

Mieke Bal also distinguishes 3 narrative positions
  • author → creator of the story
  • narrator → voice telling the story
  • focaliser → perspective from which the story is perceived (viewed/read)


Narrator

Narrator is a fictional character and is technically always in first person. There is no "And then the narrator..."
When the narrator isn't a character (internal) in the story, it is an external narrator.
When the narrator is a character in the story, it is called an internal narrator. It is part of the fabula and is defined "character-bound".


According to Mieke Bal, 1st, 2nd and 3rd person narrators don't exist, as the narrator is always "I".
However it is still useful to distinguish them:

  • 1st person narrator → tells only what they experience (limited perspective/knowledge); usually internal narrator; "I" point of view
  • 2nd person narrator → omniscent; speaking to and of others; hard to use; common in songwriting; "you" point of view
  • 3rd person narrator → usually external and omniscent and omnipresent; "they" point of view


Focalise and focalisation

Focaliser is who perceives the story. It is the character that sees and in cinema it could be the camera placement of someone's point of view.
That theorical someone is the focaliser.
Focalisation is the relation between the vision (point of view) and the object perceived. More or less it is the perspective the audience has on the story.

Creating characters

Basic character skills:

  • name
  • physicality
  • personality
  • backstory
  • world
  • unique tastes
  • ethics / beliefs

A writer that knows his characters, knows how they react under pressure.
The character's goals set the stakes for the story.
A character's want may hide their need. Unlike the wants, the need never changes. Usually.
If the want isn't the true goal, capitalize off the character's belief in a lie.


Types of characters

  • protagonist
  • antagonist
  • secondary characters

The antagonist forces the protagonist to face reality.
The main characters can speak for itself only through actions and dialogue.
The protagonist should be relatable, likeable, someone the audience mirrors to and agrees with its phylosophy.
Secondary characters
  • gives further insights on the protagonist
  • get in trouble to make the protagonist face challenges
  • assist the protagonist with skills they don't posses
  • sometimes oppositional to the protagonist (Dr. Watson vs Sherlock Holmes)


Empathizing of the character happens because he's right.


The character arc is part of the plot/fabula.
Types of character arc

  • flat arc → character has found and accepted the truth
  • negative arc → character ends up in a worse place
  • fall arc → character believes in a lie, unaware of the truth
  • corruption arc → character is aware of the truth but embraces a lie

February 13, 2026
On the 28th of December 1895 in Paris, Cinema was born.
The Lumiére brothers are the inventors of the Cinématographe: a machine with an objective (lens) that imprints light onto film stock.
In Thomas A. Edison made the kinetoscope (1889-1892). It was capable of reproducing moving film stock as well.
The kinetoscope lost to the Cinématographe because commercially it was a worse product, more expensive, for a single user while the Cinématographe allowed for more people to watch together.
Cinematography means "drawing the movement".
Eadweard Muybridge (1870-1875) was the first to capture animal movement on film.
In the following years there have been multiple machines that found other ways to solve the reproduction of a moving film stock, such as the Lanterna Magica and Mondo Nuovo.
Film stocks usually are 25mm but movies prefer a 35mm.
After the viewing of movies, the professor makes technical remarks on

  • framing
  • geometry present in the shot
  • geometry created by light
  • camera point of view
  • perspective
  • lines, shapes and the movement they create

In the beginning, films were captured at 16fps.
Cinema, being born alongside colonialism, cooperated with propaganda and helped the idea of "powerful white, western man".
Soon enough there was the transition from the cinema of attraction (1895-1905) to the cinema of narration (1905-1915).
The first narrations were cautionary tales, made to raise public awareness of dangers and espressed in a simple structure made for simple people.
One example of a simple structure is Order → Transgression → Order.
In 1896 and 1898 some films were starting to use editing.
David Wark Griffith is considered to be the first american director.

Comedians

Most important were Charlie Chaplin and Buster Keaton.
Charlie Chaplin has worked as an actor and on stage before joining Sennett's "keystone cops", a series of humorous shorts.
Charlie Chaplin acted as a tramp that is unemployed, without family, without savings and is trying to survive. The figure of the tramp is supposed to be represent an outcast, the last of the least in a violent capitalistic society. However in the fictional world where Charlie Chaplin acted, the tramp was the first amongst everybody.
Charlie Chaplin made a political statement in Modern Times, where he is a worker against the machine and revolting against the machine while bringing chaos as a comedian.
Buster Keaton was an american actor and impersonated the normal person. He was the most sophisticated comedian and film director.
While his acting was normal and his expression unfazed; the whole world around him takes life.
In cinema the continuity is needed because of different shots that are cut and put together.
Keaton reflects upon the viewer being subject to the director's choice interrupting their dreams about existing in the fictional reality representes in the film instead of their own reality.

German expressionism

1920 Exaggerating light contrasts, colours, acting, sets, lines, shapes and it made use of shadows to replace an actor's on-screen presence.
The colouring was possible by colouring the entire film stock.
The main genres it takes inspiration from are horror, thiller, science fiction.
German expressionism took heavy inspiration from its contemporary artistic movements, such as futurism.
The shapes are bent, distorted and the sceneries are unrealistic.
The camera framings were closer to the paintings german expressionism took inspiration from.
The first art piece born from expressionism is Nosferatu. Which is basically discount Dracula.
Another important film from this time is Metropolis (1927). It is a film about rich vs slaves, mann vs machine.

Noire is a french genre that worked with a german expressionism representation fo violence (use of shadows) while maintaining realistic urban settings.

French impressionism cinema

Around 1920s
Gave more iportance to stories, atmospheres and poetic vision to evoke emotions.
Key term is photogénie - photogenic. Through fading videos, overlaps and movement they intended to show the soul of a person.
Having emotions at the base of the movement, it is origin of the trascendental cinema - the passage from reality to a spiritual world.

The Passion of Joan of Arc (1928) was an avant gard film that used only close ups and detail shots.
The director, Carl Theodor Dreyer, is considered to be one of the founders of the trascendental cinema.
This film has made use of effects from the soviet avant gard, german expressionism and french impressionism for its trascendental experience.

The films with audio were called Talkies.
Some genres were born and some changed because of sound. Those genres are musical, comedy, gangster.
In the american cinema, before the 1930s, many films were cherishing the roaring twenties. Instead of the tramp, there were the mama's boys.

The Hollywood Studio System

1930 - 1960


The production company studios were in charge and in full control of

1) production → the making of a film and every role involved in the process
2) distribution → physical distribution of the final cut
3) exibition → theaters that projected the movies

In 1949, through the Paramount Case, the American governament sued a production company for the vertical integration system that created a monopoly. The production studio companies were limited to control production and distribution, leaving the exibition to third parties.
The 5 most important production companies were:
  • Paramount
  • MGM
  • 20th Century - Fox
  • Warner Bros.
  • RKO

Production code of the Motion Picture Producers and Distributors of America (MPPDA).
It is a set of rules to self censor to prevent governament censorship.
It outlines rules for controversial topics such as violence, sex, crime, etc.

Poetic realism

France, 1930 - 1940
Is about everyday life, common struggles like unemployment and violence.
It is realistic but very dreamy and aesthetic. They used real sets.
The dreams represent the character's mind and spirit.
Makes use of internal editing and of the [fr] plan sequence → [it] piano sequenza → [en] long shot → a single shot that shows many sequences.


Neorealism

Italy, 1945 - 1950
Took direct inspiration from the french poetic realism.
Real sets, showed normal people, few professional actors, everyday situations portraied faithfully without cutting out the boring parts.
It showed the miserable life after WWII and the rampant unemployment.

Definitions

Total shot → framing containing the whole scene
Crosscutting → to cut between multiple scenes happening in different locations or times
Eyeline effect → to show what a character is looking at
Slapstick → from the italian Batacchio, slapstick is two pieces of wood that upon light impact produce a loud noise used for comedic effect.
Montage → french for editing
Kuleshov effect → create meaning established by two unrelated images showed in succession. E.g. a person and a dish giving the sense of hunger
Symbolic connection/symbolic editing → using scenes or elements to symbolize an event
Schüfftan process → the illusion of huge scenes, by placing a partial mirror in front of or behind the actors that projects a miniature set
Vertical integration system → the union of film making, distribution and projection under a studio
A picture → big budget films, equivalent to today's blockbusters
B picture → low budget films, occasionally used as financial backing for producing A picture films
Internal editing → not actual editing but allowing the eye to wonder around the frame
Camera-stylo → use of the camera "like a pen"
Candid camera → when someone doesn't know that they are being recorded
